# Human Copy Style

Write like a clear person with taste and context, not like a model trying to sound helpful.

## Goals

- Sound direct, specific, and useful.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over abstractions.
- Vary sentence length. Some sentences should be short.
- Use contractions when they fit the voice.
- Cut throat-clearing, repeated framing, and summary fluff.

## Avoid AI Tells

- Do not use em dashes. Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead.
- Do not use emojis.
- Avoid filler transitions like "moreover," "furthermore," and "in addition."
- Avoid generic hype like "game-changing," "robust," "seamless," "cutting-edge," and "next-level."
- Avoid empty encouragement, fake warmth, and polished corporate uplift.
- Avoid symmetrical three-part slogans unless the user explicitly wants ad copy.
- Avoid generic opener lines like "Certainly," "Here is a polished version," or "Whether you're looking to..."

## Style Rules

- Lead with the point.
- Keep paragraphs tight.
- Prefer one idea per sentence when clarity improves.
- Use specifics from the product, audience, or page instead of generic benefits.
- If a claim could apply to any business, rewrite it.
- Read for rhythm and remove lines that sound rehearsed.
- Keep punctuation simple. Exclamation marks should be rare.

## Rewrite Pass

1. Remove filler, self-reference, and repeated setup.
2. Replace generic claims with concrete details.
3. Break up runs of same-length sentences.
4. Replace em dashes with standard punctuation.
5. Delete emojis and decorative symbols.
6. Stop once the copy sounds natural. Do not over-polish it.
